Understanding Your Loan Fundamentals

Demystifying essential financial concepts to help you borrow with confidence.

What is the Reducing Balance Method and how does it work?

In a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated solely on the outstanding principal at the beginning of each payment cycle, rather than on the original loan amount. As you make each monthly payment, a portion repays the accrued interest and the remainder decreases the principal balance. In subsequent months, you pay interest on this reduced balance, which means the interest component steadily shrinks while the principal repayment accelerates.

Why is interest front-loaded in long-term loans like Home Loans?

During the initial years of a loan, your outstanding loan principal is at its maximum. Because interest is charged on this large balance, a major share of your monthly EMI goes towards interest payments and only a minor portion reduces the principal. As the principal balance lowers over time, the interest fraction drops and principal reduction speeds up exponentially.

How does making partial prepayments save massive interest?

Every extra rupee paid goes 100% towards the loan principal. By diminishing the principal earlier than scheduled, you eliminate all future compounding interest that would have otherwise accumulated on that amount across the remaining years. Prepaying early in the tenure yields the highest compounding savings.

What is the mathematical EMI formula used by banks?

The Equated Monthly Installment is calculated as:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)

Where P = Principal Loan Amount, r = Monthly Interest Rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n = Total Number of Monthly Installments.
